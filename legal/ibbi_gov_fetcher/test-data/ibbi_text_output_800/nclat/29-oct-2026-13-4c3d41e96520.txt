Debtor(CD).
2.
Brief facts necessary for disposal of this Appeal are that the CD was in the
business of manufacture and production of fabric and textile goods. CD was admitted
into the CIRP vide order dated 29.09.2017 passed by the Adjudicating Authority in
CP (IB) No.67/CHD/PB/2017 under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy
Code,2016(Code). The appellant is the Operational Creditor and supplier of cotton to

[PAGE 2]
2

the CD, which is the raw material for CD and his claim of Rs.3,01,90,402/- was duly
admitted by the RP in relation to CIRP of the Corporate Debtor pertaining to the
supplies made during pre-CIRP period. The CD was a going concern and was
maintained as such by the RP post-admission of the CIRP. RP requested appellant
to continue to supply cotton for continuous manufacturing process of CD. The
supplies to be made was to be treated as CIRP cost and timely payments of such
supplies was assured. Appellant continued to supply raw material during the CIRP
period i.e., from 29.09.2017 to 08.08.2018. The liquidation process of the CD was
initiated vide order passed on 08.08.2018.
3.
It is claimed by the Appellant that the last supply to the CD was made on
28.09.2018, on which date after getting the audit conducted, Rs.6,95,24,683/- was
found to be due and payable by the CD to the Appellant. In the CA No.739/2019
moved by the appellant before Adjudicating Authority it is claimed that the Corporate
Debtor owed an amount of Rs.7,19,65,695/- towards the supply of raw material by
the Applicant during the of CIRP period besides the admitted amount of claim as
Operation Creditor of Rs.3,01,90,402/-. The Appellant claimed to have supplied raw
materials worth Rs.3,34,48,392/- during the liquidation. The appellant was paid,
Rs.2,00,00,000/- towards the raw materials supplied during the CIRP process and
Rs.1,40,00,000/- towards the raw materials supplied during the liquidation process
and the CD owed Rs.5,19,65,695/- on account of the pending CIRP costs of raw
materials supplied during the CIRP and Rs.1,94,00,000/- on account of the raw
materials supplied during the liquidation process and Rs. 2,29,56,480.20/- on
account of interest.

into consideration. Therefore, the auditor has taken all the documents
available with the CD in consideration, in order to determine the real amount

33.
We also notice that the Learned Adjudicating Authority has held that
there was no stipulation of payment of interest in the invoices and this
proposition has not been denied by the appellant and the appellant has not
claimed interest on the basis of any provision provided in the invoices but the
interest has been claimed on account of the fact that the amount liable to be
paid to the appellant and other operational creditors was lying in the bank in
the shape of fixed deposit and was earning interest and the CD was also
showing interest accrued on the amount payable to the operational creditors.
Therefore, we do not find any fault in the conclusions drawn by the Learned
Adjudicating Authority that no invoices have been produced which could
show that any interest on delay payment was to be paid to the appellant and
there is no existence of any separate agreement between the parties which
may allow the claim of interest of the appellant.
34.
We also notice that the interest is also being claimed by the appellant
on the basis of a circular issued by the Indian Cotton Association Limited for
the year 2018-2019 and the same has been reproduced by the Learned

Adjudicating Authority has noted that the above circular neither contains any
number or date, nor the signature of the person who has issued it, and also
that the appellant has failed to show the authority of the above Association to
set any practice or procedure and also the precedent in this regard,
particularly if it is a legislation making body under any enactment or
recognized by the IBBI or has any mention in the IBC and therefore came to
a conclusion that it is not inclined to accept the above circular submitted by
the Appellant in support of his claim for the interest. Having considered all

conferred on the Association to issue a circular of this nature.

37.
The Counsel for the appellant has also relied on Abhijit Guhathakurata
(supra), the said case was decided on its own facts and circumstances, and
the appellant due to factual difference cannot take any benefit of the same.
38.
Likewise, Shailesh Verma (supra) is a case where the payment
pertaining to the electricity dues was in dispute, and the Appellate Tribunal
directed to pay the dues within ninety days. However, the appellant has also
been given its dues and therefore cannot take any benefit of this decision.
39.
The appellant has also relied on M/s Associated Construction and
NTPC versus Resmi Constructions, Builder and Contractors (supra). M/s
Associated Construction was with regard to a case wherein the no dues
certificate in the facts of that case was issued under compulsion, which is not
a case in the case at hand. Likewise, in NTPC versus Resmi Chopra, it was

[PAGE 16]
16

held that when the rights and obligations of the parties are worked out, the
contract does not come to an end for the purpose of determination of the
disputes arising thereunder and thus the Arbitration Agreement can be
invoked. We also do not have any different opinion then formulated in this
precedent. However, we don't know in what manner the Appellant can get any
benefit of this law.
40.
Having considered all the facts and circumstances of this case, and for
the reasons given herein before, we do not find any good ground to interfere
in the impugned judgment passed by the Ld. Adjudicating Authority. In result
we do not find any merits in the Appeal and the same is hereby dismissed.

There are no orders as to cost. The pending IAs are also disposed of.
