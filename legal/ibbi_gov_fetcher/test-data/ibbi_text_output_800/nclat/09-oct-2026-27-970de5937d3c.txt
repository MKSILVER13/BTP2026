1.
This application is filed by M/s Agrifriend Ventures LLP,
through its Designated Partner Mr. Pankaj Dubey (the
“Applicant”), under Section 60(5) of the Insolvency and
Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (the “Code”) read with Regulation
32(e) and Regulation 32A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy
Board of India (Liquidation Process) Regulations, 2016 (the
“Liquidation Regulations”). The Applicant has bought STL
Exports Limited (the “Corporate Debtor”) as a going concern in
its liquidation and seeks certain reliefs and concessions. The
Respondent is the Liquidator of the Corporate Debtor.
FACTS OF THE CASE:
2.
Rashnidhi Kumar & Bros. filed an application under Section 9
of the Code before this Tribunal’s Bench at Ahmedabad,
registered as CP(IB) No. 426 of 2018. It was admitted on
07.07.2020 and Mr. Sajjan Kumar Dokania was appointed
Interim Resolution Professional (Annexure A-1). By order
dated 11.08.2023 in IA No. 186(MP)/2021, this Tribunal
ordered liquidation of the Corporate Debtor and appointed Mr.
Anil Agrawal as the Liquidator (Annexure A-2).
3.
Under Section 35(1)(f) of the Code read with Regulation 32A of
the Liquidation Regulations, the Liquidator issued e-auction
notices dated 08.06.2024, 07.08.2024 and 09.09.2024 for
sale of the Corporate Debtor as a going concern, on an ‘as is

ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS
11. We have heard the counsel for the Applicant and the
Liquidator-in-Person, and have perused the record.
12. The liquidation was ordered by this Tribunal, and the sale was
made by the Liquidator appointed by it. The application arises
out of that liquidation and is maintainable under Section 60(5)
of the Code. The Liquidator has no objection, and no objection
to the sale or the Sale Deed has been placed before us by
anyone. As noted in our order dated 13.05.2026 in IA No.

2(MP)/2026, this Tribunal, by order dated 10.12.2025 in IA
No. 219(MP)/2025, recorded that the liquidation process had
been completed, the sale proceeds had been distributed and
the liquidation bank account had been closed. Regulations
32(e) and 32A of the Liquidation Regulations were omitted with
effect from 14.10.2025. The sale to the Applicant was
completed on 15.01.2025 and this application was filed in
September 2025, before the omission, so the application is
decided on those Regulations as they stood at the time of the
sale.
13. In Punjab National Bank V. Perfect Day Inc. Company
Appeal (AT)(Ins.) No. 1427 of 2022, the NCLAT held at para
15 that where the corporate debtor is sold as a going concern,
the successful auction purchaser is entitled to certain reliefs
and concessions to run it as a going concern. We therefore
take each relief on its own, on the terms of the Sale Deed and
on whether it affects persons who are not before us.
14. Relief A. Clause 1 of the Sale Deed transfers the entire entity
of the Corporate Debtor to the Buyer by extinguishing the
entire shareholding and issuing and allotting 1,50,000 fully
paid-up equity shares of Rs. 10 each, aggregating Rs.
15,00,000/-, towards the consideration paid by the Buyer.
Relief A is in line with this clause. The Sale Deed does not
name Mr. Pankaj Dubey or Ms. Vijaya Dubey as allottees, and
the consideration was paid by the Applicant. The shares are
therefore to be allotted to the Applicant. The request for

deemed compliance with Sections 42 and 62(1)(c) of the
Companies Act, 2013 is not repeated in the written
submissions and is not considered.
15. Relief B. Clause 2 of the Sale Deed gives the Buyer the rights,
and the obligations, of a shareholder under the Companies
Act, 2013 from the date of the deed. Reconstitution of the
Board follows from this, and is allowed subject to the
formalities under that Act.
16. Relief C. This seeks relief from filing balance sheets and
annual returns, and is a direction against the Registrar of
Companies, who is not a party. It is not granted. The Applicant
may approach the Registrar in accordance with law.
17. Relief D. Clause 1 of the Sale Deed conveys the Corporate
Debtor with its licences, concessions and other rights. Its
existing licences, approvals and registrations therefore
continue with the Corporate Debtor, subject to the conditions
of the statute under which each was issued. Where a statute
requires a fresh application or compliance, the Applicant must
make it. The request that additional approvals be granted
immediately is a direction against authorities who are not
parties, and is not granted.
18. Relief E. The entire entity was sold as a going concern. The
assets therefore continue to vest in the Corporate Debtor, and
the Rs. 15,00,000/- paid by the Applicant is the consideration
under the Sale Deed.

19. Reliefs F and H. Clause 2 of the Letter of Intent dated
26.12.2024 (Annexure A-4) records that the seller will give all
due cooperation to get the transfer done as a going concern
and to change the company from ‘under liquidation’ to ‘Active’

20. Relief G. This asks that the acquisition be treated as a
Resolution Plan so that tax losses can be carried forward
under Section 79 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. No resolution

Income Tax authorities are not parties. Whether Section 79
applies is for those authorities under that Act. The relief is not
granted.
21. Relief I. In view of the orders dated 10.12.2025 in IA No.
219(MP)/2025 and 13.05.2026 in IA No. 2(MP)/2026, this
relief has become infructuous and needs no further decision.

23. Relief M. Ghanashyam Mishra and Vaibhav Goel concern
resolution plans approved under Section 31 of the Code, where
the successful applicant starts on a clean slate. In Amit
Kumar Rishi Kumar Bhabhda v. Amit Chandrashekhar
Poddar, Company Appeal (AT)(Ins.) No. 221 of 2026, the
NCLAT held that the clean slate theory is available during
liquidation where the corporate debtor is sold as a going
concern, so that a past liability left unclaimed in the liquidation
does not survive the sale. It also held that for waivers and
concessions the purchaser has to approach the concerned
statutory authorities. In Shantech International Pvt. Ltd. v.
Devendra Singh, Liquidator of Venus Rolling Mills Pvt.
Ltd., Company Appeal (AT)(Ins.) No. 1520 of 2024, the
NCLAT held that claims are filed as on the liquidation
commencement date under Regulations 12 and 16 of the
Liquidation Regulations and are dealt with under Section 53,
and that there can be no extinguishment of claims up to the
date of sale. It also approved leaving the purchaser to apply to
the authorities for waiver of electricity and water dues. Here

the Corporate Debtor was sold as a going concern without
liability, and the proceeds have been distributed (para 12). No
e-auction notice or process document has been filed with the
application, and neither the Letter of Intent nor the Sale Deed
fixes a cut-off date. The cut-off therefore rests on Regulations
12 and 16. Claims relating to the period up to 11.08.2023,
the date of the liquidation order, have been dealt with in the
liquidation and cannot be enforced against the Applicant or
the Corporate Debtor. We don’t go further. A direction on
liabilities after that date, or a declaration of clean title over the
assets, is not needed for the sale to work and would affect
persons who are not before us.
