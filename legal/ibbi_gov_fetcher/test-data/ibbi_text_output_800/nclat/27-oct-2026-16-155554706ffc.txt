ORDER

JUSTICE YOGESH KHANNA, OFFICIATING CHAIRPERSON

The instant Appeal is preferred under Section 61(1) of the Insolvency and
Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (”IBC”) against an Impugned Order dated 06.02.2026

[PAGE 2]
2

(“Impugned Order”) passed by the Ld. National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai
Bench (“NCLT”) in Interlocutory Application No. (IBC)/5600(MB)/2025 (“IA”) filed
by the Respondent in C.P. (IB) No. 37/MB/2025, whereby the Respondent Bank
under Section 95(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (“IBC”) sought
initiation of Insolvency Resolution Process against the Appellant, being a
Personal Guarantor to the Corporate Debtor i.e. M/s Frost International Limited
and the application urges the same has been erroneously admitted.
2.
The Financial Creditor had sanctioned working capital facilities to the
Corporate Debtor in the year 2014 under consortium arrangements.
Supplemental Working Capital Consortium Agreement and the Deed of
Guarantee(s) were executed on 12.05.2014 and Supplement Joint Deed of
Hypothecation on 23.05.2015. The alleged renewal of the Deed of Guarantee is
stated to have taken place on 26.11.2015.
3.
It is argued the debt is claimed to have become due on 17.04.2018 and
the account of the Corporate Debtor was classified as NPA on 17.07.2018.
However, Application under Section 95 of the Code, came to be filed only on
08.11.2024, i.e., more than six years after the alleged date of default. The
limitation prescribed under Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963 is three years.
4.
It is argued the Appellant had specifically raised the objection of limitation
before the Ld. Tribunal, contending the application was hopelessly time-barred
and not maintainable. However, the Ld. Tribunal failed to record any categorical
finding on limitation and mechanically proceeded to admit the petition under

[PAGE 3]
3

Section 100 of the IBC, primarily relying upon the Resolution Professional’s
report without independently determining the jurisdictional issue of limitation.
5.
It is argued the limitation goes to the root of maintainability and cannot
be bypassed in insolvency proceedings and thus the Impugned Order permits
revival of a time-barred claim, which is impermissible in law and the Impugned
Order therefore suffers from patent illegality and non-application of mind.
6.
Being aggrieved by the Impugned Order dated 06.02.2026, the Appellant
has preferred the present Appeal seeking setting aside of the said Order and
dismissal of the Section 95 application as barred by limitation.
7.
We have heard the arguments advance by both the counsel. We have also
perused the impugned order. The finding given in the impugned order are as
under: -
29. The Financial Creditor has filed this Petition on the basis of
default said to have occurred on 17.07.2018, which appears to
be date of classification of account of principal borrower as
NPA. The date of default qua corporate debtor is distinct than
the date of default qua personal guarantor in case of on
demand guarantee as the default qua personal guarantor
occurs upon expiry of period stated in the notice calling upon
the personal guarantor to pay in terms of the personal
guarantee, which in present case is Invocation Notice dated
19.01.2019 issued under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act
requiring the Corporate Debtor and the Personal Guarantor(s),
including the Personal Guarantor herein, to pay the outstanding
dues within 60 days of receipt of said notice.
30. In terms of notice dated 19.01.2019 the limitation period
starts from the expiry of the 60 days period i.e., 29.03.2019.
The three years limitation for filing the captioned Petition ends
on 28.03.2022. The period from 15.03.2020 to 28.02.2022 is to
be excluded in view of decision in the case of Suo Moto WP
(Civil) No. 3 of 2020, as further explained in the decision in case
of M/s Arif Azim Co. Ltd. Vs M/s Aptech Ltd. (2004) 3 S.c.R. 73:
2004 INSC155, holding at Para 84 that '"the effect of the above-
referred order of this Court in the fact of the present case is that

[PAGE 4]
4

the balance limitation left on 15.03.2020 would become
available w.e.f. 01.03.2022. The balance period of limitation
remaining on 15.03.2020 can be calculated by computing the
number of days between 15.03.2020 and 27.03.2021, which is
the day when the limitation period would have come to an end
under ordinary circumstances. The balance period thus comes
to 1 year to 13 days. This period of 1 year 13 days become
available to the Petitioner from 01.03.2022, thereby meaning
that the limitation period available to the petitioner for invoking
arbitration proceedings would have come to an end on
13.03.2023". In other words, the limitation period would stop to
run from 15.3.2020 and shall again start running from
1.3.2022. Accordingly, the balance period of 23 months 13
days available on 15.3.2020 shall start from 1.3.2022, which
takes the limitation to 12.03.2024. This Petition has been filed
on 8.11.2024. 31. Hon’ble NCLAT in Shankar Khandelwal Vs.
Omkara Asset Reconstruction Pvt. Ltd and Another Company
Appeal (AT) (Ins) No. 293 of 2025 dated 15.10.2025 has held
that “In the end, the first impression which the appellant’s
counsel could make, on a deeper scrutiny turns out to be
unsustainable. This tribunal holds that that the admission of
the Claim by the RP in the first CIRP against the CD on
22.05.2022 constituted a valid acknowledgement and its
subsequent updating on 21.02.2024 constituted the second
acknowledgement, and if terminus a quo is reckoned from any
of these dates, then both the petitions laid by the respondent
are validly instituted as the debts are not time barred on the
respective dates when they were so instituted.
32. In the present case, the CIRP in case of Corporate Debtor
commenced on 9.2.2023 pursuant to an application filed by
Bank of India. Further, we note that the claim of the financial
creditor herein was verified by the Resolution Professional in
CIRP of Corporate Debtor and was admitted. Accordingly, the
liability due to the financial creditor has been verified by the RP
of the Corporate Debtor in its CIRP as within limitation in itself
constitutes acknowledgement by the Corporate Debtor and
binds the Guarantor as well thereby further extending the
period of limitation in view of aforesaid judicial precedent.
Accordingly, we are of considered view that the present petition
is within limitation period and is maintainable.
33. The Financial Creditor issued a Demand notice dated
01.12.2023 under Rule 7(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy
(Application to Adjudicating Authority for Insolvency Resolution
Process of Personal Guarantors to Corporate Debtors) Rules,
2019 calling upon the Personal Guarantor(s) to pay the unpaid
debt in default.

[PAGE 5]
5

34. Upon perusal of the documents on record, it is clearly
established that the Corporate Debtor has committed default in
repayment of loan amount granted by the Financial Creditor.
Personal Guarantor(s) of Frost International Limited has also
committed default in repayment of the loan demanded by the
Financial Creditor after invocation of Personal Guarantee. The
Petition filed by the Financial Creditor satisfies the requirement
asset out in Section 95 of the Code.

8.
On perusal of the impugned order we find the Ld. NCLT has held the
admission of the claim of a creditor by the Resolution Professional would amount
to an acknowledgment of debt and would increase the limitation for filing the
petition. The Ld. NCLT has heavily relief upon Shankar Khandelwal Vs Omkara
Asset Reconstruction Pvt. Ltd. and Another, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins)
No.293/2025 decided on 15.10.2025 by this Tribunal to come to such
conclusion.
9.
However, the Ld. NCLT has failed to take note of the fact the said
judgement has been set aside by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal Nos.
13158-13159 of 2025 decided on 29.04.2026 wherein the Hon’ble Supreme
Court has held as under:-
16. The third issue pertains to legal character of the admission
of a claim by the IRP/RP and whether such admission can be
construed as admission of liability so as to extend the period of
limitation under Section 18 of the 1963 Act. At the outset, it
must be noted that scope and ambit of Section 18 of the 1963
Act are well-settled. For a writing to constitute a valid
acknowledgment, it must be made by the party against whom
the right is claimed, or by a person duly authorized on its
behalf; it must be made before the expiration of the prescribed
period of limitation; and, most importantly, it must evince a
conscious and unequivocal intention to admit a subsisting jural
relationship and an existing liability. A mere reference to a past
transaction or a bald recital of a debt, without an intention to
admit liability, would not suffice. The said principle has been

[PAGE 6]
6

authoritatively enunciated by this Court6. The provisions of the
Code and the Regulations were considered by this Court7 and
it has been held that RP has no adjudicatory powers and his
role involves collation of claims. RP performs its administrative
duties under Section 18 of the Code. The admission of a claim
by RP is merely an administrative/clerical task performed as
part of its statutory duties under Section 18 of the Code8 and,
therefore, admission of claim by RP only means induction/entry
of a claim. An admission of a claim by RP is akin to mere
recital/reference of debt, which does not amount to an
acknowledgment under Section 18 of the 1963 Act9. Therefore,
IRP’s admission of secured financial creditors debt in first CIRP
was not an acknowledgement under Section 18 of 1963 Act.
Accordingly, third issue is answered.
17.
It
is
a
well-settled
legal
proposition
that
an
acknowledgment under Section 18 of the 1963 Act can only
extend/renew a limitation period which has not already
expired. Therefore, a limitation period can be extended only by
an acknowledgment which is made within the period of
limitation. In any case, the admission of claim of secured
creditor by IRP on 02.05.2022 does not enure to the benefit of
the secured financial creditor as the same was not made
within the period of limitation.
10.
Per the law laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court while setting aside
the judgement in Shankar Khandelwal (supra) and also considering the facts on
records, we are satisfied the Company Petition was well beyond the period of
limitation as is also stated in the impugned order itself in its para 30 above,
where it says the limitation actually expired on 12.03.2024 and the petition was
filed much beyond that i.e. 08.11.2025. Thus we admit the appeal and set aside
the impugned order by saying the petition filed was much beyond the period of
limitation and the admission of a claim by the Resolution Professional shall not
amount to an acknowledgement of debt per Section 18 of the Limitation Act. The
Company Petition i.e. C.P. (IB) No. 37/MB/2025 is thus dismissed.
11.
With the above observations, the appeal stands disposed of.
