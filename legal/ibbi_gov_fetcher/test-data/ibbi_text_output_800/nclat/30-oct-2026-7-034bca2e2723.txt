O R D E R
(Hybrid Mode)
[Per: Justice Mohd. Faiz Alam Khan, Member (Judicial)]
Instant appeal has been filed by the Appellant against the impugned
order dated 14/09/2026 passed by the National Company Law Tribunal,
Ahmedabad Bench 1, in IA No. 1349 of 2026, moved in CP (IB) No. 195 of
2025, by which an independent forensic auditor has been appointed by the
Ld. Adjudicating Authority to examine the relevant books of accounts, ledger
entries, vouchers, and accounting records (including underlying electronic
computerized data and email trail) with regard to the security deposit of Rs.
1,34,148,00/- pertaining to the Appellant.
2.
Ld. Counsel for the Appellant submits that the Appellant is a listed,
RBI-regulated NBFC, and its shares are traded on the stock exchange. The
CD and the Appellant had entered into an operating lease agreement dated

[PAGE 2]
2

24/3/2023 for the supply of 100 electric vehicles to the CD and had also
executed a connected purchase agreement whereby the cars would be
purchased by the CD post expiry of the term of the lease under Clause 5(d) of
the agreement. The CD had paid Rs. 13,414,800/- as non-refundable
payment security, adjustable by the Appellant against the due amount.
3.
It is further submitted that from March 2025, the CD defaulted on the
payments, and a notice dated 13/4/2025 was issued by the Appellant.
Thereafter, the security deposit of Rs. 13,414,800/- was forfeited and
recorded in the ledger, and the agreement was terminated via communication
dated 21/4/2025 and termination letter dated 22/4/2025 and 92 vehicles
out of 100 vehicles were repossessed by the appellant.
4.
It is further submitted that the CIRP of the corporate debtor was
commenced on 13/06/2025, and the respondent was appointed RP. The
Appellant filed its claim on 10/07/2025, but in the claim verification
correspondence with the RP, the Appellant had inadvertently written
21/07/2025 instead of 16/04/2025 as the date of adjustment of the security
deposit. Thereafter, the Appellant filed the revised claim on 20.03.2026.
5.
It is further submitted that thereafter the respondent RP has filed an
application being IA No. 1349 of 2026 under Section 60(5) of the Code, seeking
refund of Rs. 1,341,4800 and consequential modification of the Appellant's
Form C and also sought appointment of an independent forensic auditor,
relying on a prior order of the learned adjudicating authority in the
proceedings carried out in the group entity of the CD (i.e., I.A. No. 574 of
2026). By passing the impugned order, the adjudicating authority observed
that the actual date of adjustment required factual verification and appointed

[PAGE 3]
3

an independent forensic auditor to inspect the Appellant's books, ledgers,
vouchers, and other records.
6.
It is vehemently submitted that the direction for a forensic audit travels
beyond the jurisdiction conferred under Section 60 (5) of the Code. This is
because the jurisdiction available with the adjudicating authority is over
claims and questions arising out of or in relation to the insolvency resolution
of the CD only, however, it has no jurisdiction to subject an independent third
party to a forensic examination of its books and electronic records.
7.
It is further submitted that previously a wrong order has been passed
by the learned adjudicating authority, may not be an excuse to justify the
passing of instant illegal order. Moreover, the factual situation in the earlier
case was distinguishable from the facts of the instant case.
8.
It is further submitted that no prayer for any forensic audit was sought
in the application filed by the RP and it is only by filing the rejoinder that the
RP for the very first time, introduced new relief of forensic audit, which is not
maintainable. Otherwise also, the facts and circumstances of this case do not
justify the appointment of a forensic auditor only to assess a transaction held
between the parties.
9.
It is further submitted that the interim order has serious and
disproportionate consequences for the Appellant and would also be
detrimental to the business secrecy and other confidential material (which is
usually kept by the companies). Therefore, the interim order is not
sustainable and is required to be set aside.
10.
Learned Counsel for the Respondent RP, on the other hand, submits
that it was stated in the reply filed by the Appellant before the Learned

[PAGE 4]
4

Adjudicating Authority that the security deposit was adjusted by them on
April 16, 2025, which was prior to the initiation of the corporate insolvency
resolution process of the CD (which was initiated on June 13, 2025) and thus
the Appellant has made a prayer for the forensic audit in its rejoinder.
11.
It is further submitted that Appellant has earlier informed via email
dated 06.12.2025 that the security deposit, as deposited by the CD, was
adjusted on July 21, 2025, after the commencement of the CIRP. However,
contrary to this position, in the email dated 23 December 2025, Appellant
informed that such appropriation of the security deposit was made before
initiation of the CIRP on 16.04.2025 and in this background, the Appellant
was compelled to make the prayer for the forensic audit.
12.
It is further submitted that in the meantime, in Indian Renewable
Energy Development Agency Limited vs Gensol EV Lease Limited, IA No. 574
of 126 moved in CP (IB) No. 199 of 2026, the adjudicating authority passed a
similar order against the Appellant, directing the appointment of a forensic
auditor, which was never challenged by the Appellant and the Appellant also
accepts the jurisdiction and power of the adjudicating authority in passing
such directions, including the appointment of a forensic auditor.
13.
It is further submitted that the Ld. Adjudicating Authority has
jurisdiction to pass such directions for an independent forensic audit in case
of dispute with regard to the date on which the security has been appropriated
by the Appellant and the only fact that, in the original application, no such
prayer was made is inconsequential. Moreover, the forensic audit is necessary
in the facts and circumstances of this case to assess as to on what day the
security has been appropriated by the Appellant.

[PAGE 5]
5

14.
It is further submitted that the claim of the Appellant that the security
deposit was adjusted prior to the initiation of the CIRP is not correct, as the
security deposit had not even matured on April 16, 2025, since it was
contingent and dependent upon the valid termination of the operating lease
agreement. The Appellant itself sought to terminate the operating lease
agreement via its communication dated 21/04/2025. Significantly the email
dated 21/04/2025 was not addressed to the Corporate Debtor nor to its
authorized representative and the same was addressed to a representative of
Blue Smart Charger Private Limited, and a copy of the same was sent to Mr.
Anmol Jaggi and other individuals who are not parties to the operating lease
agreement.
15.
Thus, without a valid termination of the operating lease agreement, the
Appellant's discretionary right of adjustment of the security deposit was not
matured under the operating lease agreement even on 16 April 2025.
16.
It is further submitted that, in view of the above facts and
circumstances, there is no illegality in the order passed by the lender
adjudicating authority. The appeal filed by the Appellant is devoid of force and
is liable to be dismissed as such.
17.
Having heard learned counsel for the parties and having perused the
record, it is evident that interlocutory application bearing number 1349 of
2026 was filed by the RP before the learned adjudicating authority with the
following prayers:
“a. Allow the present Application
b. Direct the Respondent, Mufin Green Finance Limited, to
forthwith refund the Security Deposit of INR 1,34, 14,800/-
(Rupees One Crore Thirty-Four Lakhs Fourteen Thousand
and Eight Hundred only) to the Corporate Debtor, Gensol

[PAGE 6]
6

Engineering Limited, along with interest thereon at the
prevailing rate from the date of adjustment, i.e., July 21,
2025, till the date of actual refund; and

c. Direct the Respondent to file a revised I amended Fom 1
C before the Resolution Professional, duly reflecting the
correct outstanding amount after giving full credit for the
Security Deposit amount of INR 1,34,14,800/- already
received and retained by the Respondent;

d. Pass such other and further order(s) as this Hon'ble
Tribunal may deem fit and proper in the facts and
circumstances of the present Application and in the interests
of justice”.

18.
It appears to be an admitted fact that the CIRP against the CD Gensol
Engineering Limited was initiated by an order passed by the Learned
Adjudicating Authority on 13.06.2025, on a petition filed under Section 7 of
the Code.
19.
It is further reflected and is also admitted by the parties that on
24/03/2023, the corporate debtor and the Appellant executed the Electric
Vehicles Services Agreement and Purchase Agreement for 100 electric vehicles
and services. The CD paid Rs. 1,34,14,800/- as security deposit and the lease
agreement was to expire on 07/04/2028. According to the Resolution
Professional, the Appellant appropriated Rs. 1,34,14,800/- on 21/07/2025
during the moratorium. In this background, the resolution professional
sought claim details on 06/11/2025, and Muffin filed the claim of Rs.
19,522,187/-. The resolution professional on 25/11/2025 sought details of
the deposit, and on 6.12.2025, the muffin disclosed about the adjustment of
the security deposit on 21/07/2025 and has relied on the lease agreement.
Subsequently, the Appellant has taken the stand that the security deposit
had already been adjusted before 13 June 2025. In this regard, the

[PAGE 7]
7

communication dated 21/4/2025 and 22/04/2025 with regard to
termination of the lease agreement has been relied.
20.
We notice that the RP, in its rejoinder, has disputed the claim of the
Appellant with regard to adjustment of the security before 13 June 2025. By
filing a rejoinder, it is submitted that the actual date and manner of
adjustment could only be verified from the books of accounts, vouchers, and
other electronic audit trail of the Appellant.
21.
It is thus evident that, in view of the contradictory stand taken by the
Appellant, Ld. Adjudicating Authority thought that it would be convenient to
examine the relevant books of account, ledger entries, voucher, and electronic
computerized data, etc., of the Appellant (in order to determine as to on which
date the security deposit deposited by the CD of Rs. 1,34,14,800/-, was
confiscated/appropriated by the Appellant). The necessity, in our considered
opinion, for appointment of a forensic auditor has emerged only on account
of the fact that contradictory stand has been taken by the Appellant with
regard to the date of appropriation of the security.
22.
We also notice, and it has also been highlighted by the Respondent RP,
that on an earlier occasion also, with regard to the similar dispute in the same
CIRP, by passing an order dated 27/08/2026 in CPIB No. 199 of 2025 a
forensic auditor was directed to be appointed, and at that point of time, the
Appellant has neither challenged the jurisdiction of the Adjudicating
Authority in passing the said order nor has challenged the same by filing any
appeal. Therefore, it is contended by Learned Counsel for the RP that the
Appellant is estopped from challenging the similar order. The said order has
also been placed on record by the Counsel for the RP and has also been filed

[PAGE 8]
8

with the written submissions. The same has been passed with regard to the
return of the refundable security deposit of Rs. 10,005,971/- and a similar
stand of contradictory nature was taken by the Appellant therein. It is in the
backdrop of the facts and circumstances of that case that the Ld. Adjudicating
Authority has appointed a forensic auditor. It has been submitted by Learned
Counsel for the RP that, before any forensic audit, the amount of security
deposit in that case was paid by the Appellant.
23.
Be that as it may, it appears to be an admitted situation that the
Appellant has given two dates to the RP with regard to appropriation of the
security deposit, deposited by the CD. Though it has been argued with
considerable force on behalf of the Appellant that in the earlier
communication dated 6.12.2025, the Appellant had inadvertently written the
date of appropriation of the security deposit as 21.07.2025 instead of
16.04.2025 and in fact, the security deposit was appropriated on 16.04.2025,
and thereafter the agreement was terminated vide communication dated
21.04.2025 by email and vide letter dated 22.04.2025. But the fact remains
that the appellant had given two dates of appropriation of security, i.e., 16
April 2025 and 21 July 2025, while the date of initiation of the CIRP is 13
June 2025. Thus, in our considered opinion, due to the contradictory stand
taken by the appellant, the Ld. Adjudicating Authority has not committed any
illegality in directing the audit (only for the limited purpose of ascertaining the
actual date on which the security deposited by the CD has been appropriated
by the appellant).
24.
In view of the facts and circumstances of the case and the reasons given
hereinbefore, we do not find any illegality in the order passed by the Ld.

[PAGE 9]
9

Adjudicating Authority. In result, the appeal filed by the Appellant appears to
be without merits and is dismissed as such.
25.
There is no order as to costs. The pending I.A.’s, if any, are also disposed
of.
